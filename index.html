Chapter 1

Gaming isn't just for teenagers

Most Australian gamers are adults

82% of Australians play video games, and the average Australian gamer is 35 years old. Out of every 100 gamers, 81 of them are aged 18 or over.

That makes gaming one of the country's most common hobbies, shared across all ages. The rest of this page looks at the competitive online games that many of those players follow.

Source: IGEA and Bond University, Australia Plays 2025.

The games the world watches

Millions of people don't just play these games, they watch other people play them, live, just like traditions sports, on streaming sites like Twitch.

A decade of watching on Twitch

The thicker a game's band, the more hours people spent watching it that month. Valorant arrived in April 2020 with a huge spike, because early access to the game was handed out to people watching streams. (There is a feature on twitch called drops where people watching the stream has a chance to win the drop and gain early access to play the game such as valorant)

Chapter 3

Where the money goes

Professional players compete for prize money at tournaments known as esports. Some games pay far more than others.

Prize money, year by year

Darker squares mean more prize money, in millions of US dollars. Blank squares mean the game hadn't been released. Dota 2's total crashed in 2020 when its biggest event, The International, was cancelled because of COVID-19, then bounced straight back. (Fun fact about Dota 2 prize pool, its actually crowdfunded by viewers and sales from the game. Despite being less popular in terms of views, it always have the highest prize pools)

Source: Esports Earnings via Kaggle

Which kinds of games pay the most?

Each box is one game, sized by all the prize money it has ever paid out, and grouped by genre. A MOBA (multiplayer online battle arena) is a team game where two sides of five try to destroy each other's base. League of Legends, Dota 2 and Mobile Legends are all MOBAs, and together MOBAs have paid out more than any other genre.

Source: Esports Earnings via Kaggle, all-time totals to October 2024.

Where the winners come from

Total prize money won by each country's players. Australia ranks 14th in the world, and its top-earning game is Dota 2. Countries like the US, China, South korea have dominated the esports scene for years. Hover over a country to see its top game.

Source: Esports Earnings, accessed October 2026

What Australia's winners play

The games Australian players have won the most prize money in. Dota 2 leads by a long way, mostly thanks to one player. Fortnite is a very different story: its $4.7 million is shared among almost 1,000 Australian players, while Dota 2's $8 million went to fewer than 100.

Chapter 5

Australia up close

Every match goes through Sydney

Games like League of Legends and Valorant run on servers in Sydney, so every Australian player's data travels there and back. "Ping" is the time that round trip takes, in milliseconds (ms). In fast games, a few milliseconds can decide who wins, and players in Perth start every match behind.

Sources: ABS Regional Population 2024–25; IGEA Australia Plays 2025.

What it all means

  1. Australia plays the most popular gamesMost Australians watch, play and follow the world's most popular esports such as League of Legends, Valorant and Counter-Strike.
  2. Money and audiences tell different storiesDota 2 has paid out the most prize money, League of Legends draws the biggest crowds, and Mobile Legends fills stadiums in Southeast Asia while barely registering in Australia.
  3. Distance still mattersAustralian can hold world class events, it's just not as convenient for players to travel all the way down for them.
