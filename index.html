Chapter 1

Gaming isn't just for teenagers

Most Australian gamers are adults

82% of Australians play video games, and the average Australian gamer is 35 years old. Out of every 100 gamers, 81 are aged 18 or over.

That makes games one of the country's most common hobbies, shared across ages. The rest of this page looks at the competitive online games that many of those players follow.

Source: IGEA and Bond University, Australia Plays 2025.

Chapter 3

Where the money goes

Professional players compete for prize money at tournaments known as esports. Some games pay far more than others.

Prize money, year by year

Darker squares mean more prize money, in millions of US dollars. Blank squares mean the game hadn't been released. Dota 2's total crashed in 2020 when its biggest event, The International, was cancelled because of COVID-19, then bounced straight back.

Source: Esports Earnings via Kaggle

Which kinds of games pay the most?

Each box is one game, sized by all the prize money it has ever paid out, and grouped by genre. A MOBA (multiplayer online battle arena) is a team game where two sides of five try to destroy each other's base. League of Legends, Dota 2 and Mobile Legends are all MOBAs, and together MOBAs have paid out more than any other genre.

Source: Esports Earnings via Kaggle, all-time totals to October 2024.

Chapter 5

Australia up close

What each state is searching for

How Google searches for five popular games were split in each state over the past year. Use the menu below the map to switch games, and click a state to see its full breakdown. Dota 2 leads in every state, while Mobile Legends, huge in Southeast Asia, barely registers here.

Sources: Google Trends (Australia, October 2025 to October 2026, share of searches among the five games shown); ABS state populations, June 2025. Player numbers are estimates: population × 82% (IGEA Australia Plays 2025). Search interest shows what people look up, not how many people play each game.

Every match goes through Sydney

Games like League of Legends and Valorant run on servers in Sydney, so every Australian player's data travels there and back. "Ping" is the time that round trip takes, in milliseconds (ms). In fast games, a few milliseconds can decide who wins, and players in Perth start every match behind.

Sources: ABS Regional Population 2024–25; IGEA Australia Plays 2025. "Fastest possible ping" is the round trip at the speed of light through fibre-optic cable; real pings are always higher.

What it all means

  1. Australia plays the world's gamesMost Australians play, and the games we follow are global: League of Legends, Valorant and Counter-Strike sit near the top of what the world watches.
  2. Money and audiences tell different storiesDota 2 has paid out the most prize money, League of Legends draws the biggest crowds, and Mobile Legends fills stadiums in Southeast Asia while barely registering in Australia.
  3. Distance still mattersMelbourne can host world-class events, but every online match still runs through Sydney, and players in Perth and Darwin start every game more than 30 milliseconds behind.
